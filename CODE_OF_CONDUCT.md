
# Code of Conduct

## Our Pledge

Quality Engineering Hub is an open engineering community
dedicated to improving software quality through testing,
automation, security, performance engineering and knowledge
sharing.

We are committed to providing a welcoming, respectful,
professional and inclusive environment for everyone who
participates in our community.

We welcome contributions from people of all experience levels,
backgrounds, nationalities, cultures and professional disciplines.

Our community values technical excellence, intellectual honesty,
constructive criticism, collaboration and continuous learning.

## Our Standards

Examples of behaviour that contributes to a positive community
include:

- Being respectful, professional and considerate.
- Welcoming newcomers and helping them understand the project.
- Providing constructive and technically useful feedback.
- Respecting different opinions, experiences and approaches.
- Accepting responsibility for mistakes and learning from them.
- Giving credit to the original authors of ideas and contributions.
- Communicating disagreements professionally.
- Focusing discussions on engineering problems and solutions.
- Respecting the time and effort of other contributors.

Examples of unacceptable behaviour include:

- Harassment, discrimination or personal attacks.
- Insulting, threatening or humiliating other contributors.
- Publishing someone's private information without permission.
- Trolling, deliberate disruption or persistent bad-faith arguments.
- Sexualised language or unwanted sexual attention.
- Unprofessional conduct in public discussions or private
  community spaces.
- Deliberately intimidating or discouraging new contributors.
- Retaliating against someone who reports unacceptable behaviour.

## Technical Discussions and Disagreements

Quality Engineering Hub encourages strong technical debate.

Contributors are welcome to challenge:

- Architectural decisions.
- Testing strategies.
- Automation frameworks.
- Programming languages and tools.
- Engineering standards.
- Technical assumptions and implementation choices.

However, disagreements must remain focused on technical
evidence, reasoning and engineering outcomes.

Challenge ideas, not people.

Different technical opinions are not automatically violations
of this Code of Conduct.

## Scope

This Code of Conduct applies to all Quality Engineering Hub
community spaces, including:

- GitHub repositories.
- Issues and discussions.
- Pull requests and code reviews.
- Project documentation.
- Community communication channels.
- Public representation of the organisation.
- Other spaces officially managed by Quality Engineering Hub.

It also applies when an individual is officially representing
the organisation in public spaces.

## Responsibilities

Project maintainers are responsible for clarifying and enforcing
the standards of acceptable behaviour.

Maintainers may remove, edit or reject contributions, comments,
commits, code, issues and other interactions that violate this
Code of Conduct.

Maintainers may also temporarily or permanently restrict
participation when necessary.

## Reporting Unacceptable Behaviour

If you experience or witness unacceptable behaviour, report it
privately to the organisation maintainers.

Please include:

- A description of the incident.
- Relevant GitHub usernames or participants.
- Links to relevant discussions, issues or pull requests.
- Any additional information that may help the maintainers
  understand the situation.

Please avoid sharing sensitive personal information publicly.

Reports will be reviewed as promptly and fairly as possible.

## Enforcement Guidelines

Maintainers will consider the nature, severity and impact of
each incident.

Possible enforcement actions include:

1. Correction: A private or public warning with guidance on
   expected behaviour.
2. Warning: A formal warning explaining the consequences of
   continued unacceptable behaviour.
3. Temporary restriction: A temporary restriction from
   participating in community spaces.
4. Permanent restriction: Permanent removal from community
   participation where justified.

Enforcement decisions should be proportionate, consistent
and documented appropriately.

## Confidentiality

Reports should be handled with discretion.

Maintainers will not unnecessarily disclose the identity of
reporters or other sensitive information.

Confidentiality cannot be guaranteed in every circumstance,
particularly where further investigation or safety measures
are necessary.

## Appeals

Individuals who believe an enforcement decision was made
incorrectly may request a review by contacting the organisation
maintainers privately.

Appeals should include the relevant decision and the reasons
for requesting reconsideration.

## Community Principles

Quality Engineering Hub is built on the following principles:

- **Respect:** Treat people professionally.
- **Integrity:** Be honest about your work, findings and limitations.
- **Evidence:** Support technical claims with evidence where possible.
- **Learning:** Share knowledge and help others improve.
- **Quality:** Promote reliable, secure and maintainable software.
- **Collaboration:** Build better engineering practices together.

We believe that a strong engineering community is built through
respect, accountability, curiosity and a shared commitment to
better software.
